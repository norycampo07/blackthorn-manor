---
name: Investigation intent
description: The user's scope and discovery boundaries for focused investigation layers.
---

When an investigation replaces an existing instant discovery, do not add a duplicate room action. Preserve the original relic reward and its source inside the investigation.

**Why:** The user corrected the Library integration: the desk investigation replaces the instant Ancient Diary discovery, rather than adding a fourth choice or removing the diary.

**How to apply:** Keep the room menu's existing action count when replacing a discovery. Move its reward into the appropriate internal investigation step.

Name the room action for the initial inspection, not for a secret the player has not discovered. A hidden compartment must only become apparent through investigating the drawer.

**Why:** The user explicitly said the player should not already know there is a hidden compartment.

**How to apply:** Avoid revealing hidden mechanisms in room-action labels or descriptions. Preserve discovery order inside the focused layer.

Room art provides atmosphere; investigation assets provide interaction. The investigation should feel like leaning closer to an object while still in the same room, not opening a popup, modal, framed window, or separate page.

**Why:** This is the user's stated design philosophy for investigation scenes.

**How to apply:** Keep the dimmed, non-interactive character panel visible. Center the complete transparent investigation asset inside the room's story column, keep lore on the right (never below), and use Step Back or ESC to return.

Keep the investigation asset, hotspots, and Step Back physically anchored. Only the evidence-reading panel may scroll, in a readable, translucent dark-parchment container.

**Why:** The user confirmed the first investigation concept worked, but said whole-layer scrolling makes the desk and hotspots shift and breaks the sense of inspecting a physical object.

**How to apply:** Reserve stable artwork and right-side reading regions across phone portrait, short landscape, and desktop. Changing or scrolling evidence must not move the artwork. The lore should resemble a weathered estate archive, not clean quest paper; target 70–75% background opacity while keeping text legible.

Investigations are sources of discoveries, not a parallel reward framework. Use existing Discovery, Item, Family Fragment, and Luck presentations rather than building another queue or local acquisition notices. Collectible rewards are granted on acceptance, not on discovery.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested architecture alignment, not a Discovery-system redesign, and made acceptance timing a global rule for room gameplay, investigation, combat, and future systems. They confirmed this architecture works well and asked that UI refinements preserve it.

**How to apply:** Preserve pending rewards across reloads, keep evidence reading immediate and button-free, and let FEAR/LUCK temporarily take priority above investigation. Preserve already-earned legacy rewards.

Use two player-facing journal categories: Family Fragments for all collectible story fragments, and Investigation Notes for examined evidence. Do not expose a separate Lore Fragments category.

**Why:** The user wants one collectible family-story category while keeping investigation evidence distinct.

**How to apply:** Consolidate fragment display and counts without losing existing story text or duplicating previously earned fragments.

Bookshelf lever evidence is a future mystery setup only. Do not unlock any room or the Secret Tunnel, name the Music Room, or reveal the source of the distant piano note.

**Why:** The user explicitly wants one unexplained note beyond the Library walls, leaving its origin unresolved for future content.

**How to apply:** Future references may acknowledge the discovery, but do not turn its current investigation interaction into a progression unlock or an explanation.

Keep discovery titles distinct across notes, family fragments, items, and other discoveries.

**Why:** The user wants players to distinguish each type immediately, without overlapping names or duplicate lore entries.

**How to apply:** Check existing titles when adding investigations; replace obsolete room discoveries rather than retaining duplicate journal content.

An action's eye icon means its primary intended outcome has been obtained, not that every hotspot, note, or optional reward has been exhausted. This rule applies across room actions, investigations, and future progression systems.

**Why:** The user explicitly aligned the long-term design around meaningful progression rather than completionism.

**How to apply:** Completion belongs to the primary outcome's acceptance. Distant Note—not Burned Journal—is the Bookshelf primary outcome. Optional evidence must remain accessible after an action is explored. Keep earned legacy outcomes valid.

Preserve contextual locked-action explanations and the lock/key icon for unmet prerequisites.

**Why:** The user confirmed the existing locked-action behavior works well and wants the same discovery-focused pattern maintained in future content.

**How to apply:** Adding an investigation must not bypass its existing item prerequisite. The lock describes what is missing without explaining hidden mechanisms.

Relic investigations should differ in complexity: the Nursery has a small discovery chain; the Wardrobe is straightforward; the Cellar should make the player earn the dagger by understanding a visibly restrained weapon.

**Why:** The user wants distinct childhood, inheritance, and sacrifice experiences, not repeated nested hidden compartments. The shrine's obscured weapon need not match the final dagger artwork exactly.

**How to apply:** Avoid secret-compartment-within-secret-compartment puzzles for the Wardrobe. Treat the shrine's roots, restraints, and symbols as release conditions rather than hiding the dagger.
