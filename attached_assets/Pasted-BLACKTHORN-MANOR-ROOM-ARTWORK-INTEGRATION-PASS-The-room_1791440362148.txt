ROOM STORYTELLING SUPPORT

The room artwork should not be treated as decorative images.

These images are environmental storytelling scenes.

The UI should encourage players to study the images.

Optional:

- Slight zoom on hover
- Click-to-enlarge
- Lightbox viewing mode

Future support may include image hotspots.

Examples:

Dining Room
- Envelope
- Empty Chair
- Butler

Cellar
- Disturbed Earth
- Bottle Rack
- Chains

Nursery
- Rocking Horse
- Crib
- Doorway

Secret Tunnel
- Hidden Cache
- Footprints

Observatory
- Telescope
- Constellation Charts

Do not implement hotspots yet.

Only prepare the architecture for future support.

IMAGE SCALING

Room artwork should show the entire composition.

Many images contain important environmental details.

Avoid cropping:

- Chapel altar
- Ritual Chamber pedestal
- Cellar roots
- Observatory charts
- Nursery doorway
- Dining Room table
- Secret Tunnel footprints

MONSTER IMAGE PRESENTATION

Monster artwork should display the complete creature whenever possible.

Avoid cropping:

- Heads
- Faces
- Eyes
- Wings
- Claws
- Major silhouette details

Especially preserve full visibility for:

- Hanging Lady
- Shadow Hound
- Bat Swarm
- Lord Blackthorn Phase 1
- Lord Blackthorn Final Form

Do not crop monster heads.

Do not cut off key visual storytelling elements.

If necessary:

- Scale images down slightly
- Increase image container height
- Use contain-fit instead of cover-fit
- Prefer letterboxing over cropping

Boss and monster artwork should remain fully visible and readable.

DESIGN GOAL

The room illustrations should feel like pages from a gothic storybook.

The player should study each image for clues, mysteries, and environmental storytelling.

The artwork should become one of the primary ways players experience Blackthorn Manor.

The art is not decorative.

The art is part of the storytelling.
